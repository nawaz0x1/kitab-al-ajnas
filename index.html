About this edition

A book of names,
ranks and seals

The Kitāb al-Ajnās is a 200-page Arabic Solomonic grimoire attributed to Āṣif ibn Barkhiyā, the legendary vizier of King Solomon. It maps the unseen world as a hierarchy — from the angels around the Throne down to the kings of the jinn — and preserves the names, seals, invocations and ritual conditions attached to each rank.

What this edition does

This reader presents the complete book in English with the Arabic original alongside it. The translation follows the printed Arabic text closely, including its repetitions, ritual conditions and technical vocabulary. All content chapters have been translated and checked against the printed source. Where the source is damaged or a line is missing, the edition says so rather than inventing text.

The holy names are never translated away. Each divine name, angel, jinn king, and non-Arabic voces magicae is kept in Arabic script and supplied with an English transliteration and a Bengali transliteration. The name panels at the end of each chapter collect them in one place.

How to read it

Use the EN, ع and Both controls to choose between the English translation, the Arabic text, and a parallel view. Names with a dotted underline can be clicked for their Arabic form and pronunciation.

The page numbers in the margins refer to the 2005 Beirut printing. Talismans, magic squares and seal diagrams are described in the chapter notes where they cannot be represented adequately in plain text.

The book and its world

A hierarchy of
kinds and names

Kitāb al-Ajnās — “The Book of the Races” — belongs to the Solomonic current of Arabic esoteric writing. Its attributed author, Āṣif ibn Barkhiyā, is remembered in Islamic tradition as the courtier of Solomon who brought the throne of the Queen of Sheba “before the twinkling of an eye.” The attribution is traditional rather than documentary: the printed book is a composite that gathers older material on the seven heavens, the angelic hierarchies, the divine names, and the kings of the jinn.

Why “the races”?

The Arabic word ajnās (singular jins) means “kinds,” “classes,” or “races.” The title reflects the book’s organising idea: the invisible world is not an undifferentiated crowd of spirits but an ordered hierarchy of kinds. Each rank has its own name, seal, planet, day, metal, colour, and presiding king.

The seven heavens are arranged in the order of the classical planets, from the seventh heaven of the sun down to the lowest heaven of the moon. Beneath them stand the earthly kings and the jinn who serve them, each with a seal and a set of operations.

Why the names matter

The book is also a name-book. Its invocations preserve Arabic divine names alongside Hebrew and Aramaic theonyms — يَاهُو (Yāhū), أَهْيَا شَرَاهِيَا (Ehyeh Asher Ehyeh), أَدُونَاي (Adonai), صَبَاؤُوت (Sabaoth), and شَدَّاي (Shaddai) — together with a large repertoire of angelic and jinn names.

These names are the operating core of the text. For that reason this edition keeps them in Arabic script and gives English and Bengali transliteration rather than translating them away.

The source. The translation is made from a 200-page printed Arabic edition (Beirut, 2005). The printed source remains the authoritative witness to the Arabic text. The book’s own table of contents is included as an appendix.
